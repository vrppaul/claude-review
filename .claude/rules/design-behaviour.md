# How the page answers the reader

From the owner's design canvas of 2026-10-20. How the page looks: `.claude/rules/design.md`, which also says when a part is held to these.

## Folded and put away

- A thing that folds keeps its head the same open and folded. Folded, the head alone says what is under it — who, the first words, how many — and whatever is still happening there.
- A list whose one item fits the line it would fold to is that item, drawn whole, with nothing to fold; any other list folds to that line, cut with an ellipsis.
- Nothing folded offers to be asked about: a comment is about what the reader sees whole.
- A list a thread hangs in stays open while it does: its fold is off.
- An arrow is a line chevron: down while folded, up while open. A chevron only folds; any other icon draws the thing it names.
- A toggle's icon shows the state it is in.
- The button that puts a thing away brings it back, from the same spot.
- Both side columns are put away alike: each leaves a rail, the column narrowed, its head the same bar, its toggle at the outer edge; the rail says what is behind it.
- What is seldom read waits behind an icon, its words on the pointer. An act seldom taken, clear by its icon, is an icon with its name on the pointer.

## Words

- An act stands at what it acts on: asking the agent about what the reader wrote stands at their words.
- An act has one name on every button that does it, and what it did says the same word; two acts never share a name.
- A button says what will happen.
- What cannot be done now says why, where the act would stand.
- A failure says what went wrong and what to do, in red, under what failed; the act stays, to try again.
- What was taken back stays seen where it happened: its words struck through, and a line of its own saying what was done and when.
- What has nothing to say is not drawn: no checks reported, no chip.
- A count stands on what it counts: what goes with the round, on Send. A notification is a mark with its figure.
- The keys a field takes are said at the start of the row of its acts.
- The header says where the change is, not the tool's name.

## Asking first

- What cannot be undone asks first, on one line, in a pane at its control: the question, then keep (quiet), then go ahead (filled, in danger's red). One confirmation draws every such question.
- The question names everything that goes with it: a comment and its replies, counted.

## Motion

- Something moves only in answer to what the reader did, to show what changed.
- Nothing moves by itself: work going on breathes (its dots), and what arrived is lit by a light that fades, but never moves in.
- A fold, a switch and a change of state take 150 ms; a pane comes from its control in 120 ms and goes at once; a side column takes 200 ms.
- Nothing moves that can be huge, such as a file in the diff, nor what the keys move faster than, such as the row the reader is at.
- With less motion asked for, nothing moves.

## Narrow windows

- The header never wraps. As the window narrows, words give way to their icons, the name kept on the pointer; then what is drawn rather than said goes; the main act keeps its word and its figure to the last.
